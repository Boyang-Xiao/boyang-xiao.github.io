### The problem

Bile and pancreatic juice carry early markers of liver and pancreatic cancer, but the ducts that hold them are millimeters wide and tortuous. Capsule endoscopes are too large, and catheter-based ERCP is invasive and carries a risk of complications.

### What the capsules do

- Each capsule is **1.8 mm in diameter and 1.6 mm tall**, with flexible magnetic inlet and outlet valves and a superabsorbent polymer pump.
- Valves stay closed below 10 mT. A larger field (about 84 to 90 mT) triggers sampling, and the swollen polymer seals the sample inside.
- A rotating field of about 20 mT rolls a whole group of capsules together. A group collects more sample than one capsule and keeps working if one fails.
- A thin catheter delivers and retrieves the capsules.

### Results

- Sampled fluid in about **10 minutes**, at about **0.12 µL/min** with 120 µm inlet holes.
- Navigated a bile-duct phantom and sampled in an ex vivo porcine liver under ultrasound guidance, with X-ray imaging for tracking.
