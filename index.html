Boyang Xiao

Robotics and Medical Device Engineer  |  Ph.D. Candidate in Mechanical Engineering, Vanderbilt University

Nashville, TN

xiaoby1996@gmail.com

I design, model, integrate, and test medical robotic systems for minimally invasive care, combining magnetic actuation and sensing with controls and experimental validation. My work spans soft robotic catheters, miniature robots for targeted drug delivery and sensing, and precision surgical robotics, developed with clinicians and tested in anatomical phantoms and ex vivo tissue.

I am completing my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering in the Dong Lab at Vanderbilt University (expected January 2027) and am looking for full-time robotics, mechanical, or systems engineering roles in medical robotics and medical devices, starting February 2027. I am open to relocating anywhere in the U.S.

Left: a wheel-based magnetic soft robot driven and steered by external magnetic fields. Right: a magnetic capsule triggered by an external field to sample fluid on demand, with integrated sensing.

Selected results

  • 1.75 mm catheter, 2 mm ducts. A soft magnetic robotic catheter that navigated ducts as narrow as 2 mm and turns up to 150°, tested in a biliary-tract phantom and ex vivo porcine anatomy. More
  • Under 20 µm residual error. Calibration and compensation of a retinal surgical robot reduced tool-tip position error below 20 micrometers. More
  • Wireless, multiplexed drug delivery. Magnetic valves operating below 20 mT released two drugs independently or mixed them on demand, with consistent doses over 7 days and wireless volume readout in an ex vivo porcine bladder. More
  • Liquid handling on a 10 mm robot. Soft robots with on-board magnetic pumps and valves crawled, walked, and climbed on tissue, then sampled or released liquid on command. More

What I bring to a team

  • Model to prototype. Kinematics and mechanics models (Cosserat rod, beam theory, calibration error models) that guide device design and control.
  • Integrated systems. Actuation, sensing, software, and experimental workflows combined into working platforms, from Franka robot-arm magnetic actuation to vision-based tracking.
  • Application-driven testing. Benchtop, anatomical phantom, and ex vivo experiments designed with gastroenterologists and urologists.

Tools: C++, Python, MATLAB, ROS 1/2, CISST, SolidWorks, LabVIEW, Arduino, Franka robotic arms, 3D printing, laser micromachining.
